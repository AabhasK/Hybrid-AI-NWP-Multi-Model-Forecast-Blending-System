# Data Note

**Ground truth is real. The four contributing forecast streams are synthetic, by design.**

The verification target in this system is genuine: daily rainfall and 2 m temperature
from **ERA5 reanalysis**, pulled for 121 grid cells at 0.5° resolution across a 5° × 5°
box over Maharashtra (16–21 °N, 73–78 °E) for the 2023 southwest monsoon, 1 June to
31 August. Every skill score, RMSE, MAE and probability on the dashboard is measured
against that observed field. The four contributing model streams — a physics-based NWP
proxy, an AI/ML model proxy, an ensemble-mean proxy and a persistence baseline — are
synthetically generated by perturbing that truth with distinct, physically-motivated
error signatures: a dry bias that deepens with lead time and is partly offset over
resolved orography for the NWP proxy; near-zero bias with a flat lead-time decay but
acute fragility across monsoon regime transitions for the AI proxy; variance shrunk
toward local climatology for the ensemble mean, leaving it structurally blind to
extremes; and naive carry-forward for the baseline. Critically, the first three share a
common error component representing the analysis and initial-condition uncertainty that
every operational centre inherits from the same observing system — without it, four
independently-perturbed sources could be averaged into an implausibly perfect forecast,
and the reported blend skill would be an artefact of the simulation rather than a
property of the method. We generated these streams because time-aligned, gridded
operational output from multiple real NWP and AI weather models (IFS, GFS, NCMRWF-GFS,
GraphCast, Pangu-Weather) is not obtainable within a hackathon timeframe: the archives
are access-controlled, individually licensed, and distributed in incompatible grids and
formats. **The blending methodology is entirely source-agnostic.** The learner consumes
an arbitrary set of forecast columns alongside lead time, terrain and a regime
diagnosis, and it never inspects where a column came from. Substituting real model
output is a change to one data-loading step — replace the generated columns in
`data/forecasts.parquet` with the real ones on the same schema — and the training,
weighting, verification and alerting layers run unmodified.

## What this means for each claim

| Claim on the dashboard | Rests on | Would it survive real data? |
|---|---|---|
| Blended RMSE beats every individual source | Real ERA5 truth, synthetic sources | The *method* transfers; the *margin* would differ |
| Skill varies by lead time, regime and terrain | Real ERA5 spatial/temporal structure | Yes — this structure is observed, not injected |
| Weight maps are interpretable and sum to 1 | Constrained NNLS on the sources | Yes — solver is independent of source provenance |
| Heavy-rain flagger ROC-AUC | Real ERA5 threshold breaches as labels | Labels are real; features are partly synthetic |
| Monsoon active / break / normal regimes | Real ERA5 domain-mean rainfall anomaly | Yes — diagnosed entirely from observations |

## Honest limitations

1. **The margin is not a forecast of the margin.** With real models the blend would still
   be expected to beat its best member — that result is well established in multi-model
   post-processing — but the 14.6% RMSE reduction reported here is a property of this
   simulation's error structure, not a prediction about IFS-plus-GraphCast.
2. **Climatology uses the full season.** `clim_rain` and `clim_t2m` are computed across
   all 92 days rather than from the training folds alone. Operationally you would use a
   30-year normal, which is genuinely prior information, so we treat climatology as known
   a priori. The effect on reported scores is small but it is not exactly zero.
3. **One season, one region.** 92 days over one 5° box. Cross-regional and cross-year
   generalisation is untested.
4. **Regime diagnosis is thresholded, not dynamical.** Active/break/normal comes from a
   standardised domain-mean rainfall anomaly (±0.5 σ), not from a circulation index such
   as the monsoon Hadley index or an MJO phase.

## What is genuinely defensible here

Everything to do with *evaluation discipline*. Scores come from contiguous time-block
cross-validation rather than a random split, because adjacent days and neighbouring cells
are strongly autocorrelated and a random split would leak the answer across the fold
boundary. The regime and domain-anomaly features fed to the model are diagnosed from the
**forecast** fields available at issue time, not from the observations being predicted —
an earlier version used the truth-derived regime and had to be rebuilt once that was
recognised as target leakage. Those choices are what make the numbers mean something, and
they carry over to real data unchanged.
